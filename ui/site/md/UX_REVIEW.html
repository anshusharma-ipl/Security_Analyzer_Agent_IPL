UX review of the console and the explainer pages

Reviewed on 2026-10-27 from screenshots at 1440 px and on a phone-width window. Reviewer: Claude, acting as a UX designer. The changes below are in the repository.

Who this is for and what they need to do

Engineers and security engineers who scan a project, read each proposed fix, and decide. The job is: start a scan, understand what was found, judge each fix with confidence, and take away the report. Everything else is secondary.

What was wrong

#FindingWhy it mattersWhat changed
1The review was one long scroll: stepper, review box, findings, trace table and log stacked in one column.The person decides on one finding at a time, but had to scroll past everything else to reach the decision.A review desk: findings on the left, the selected finding on the right, with the decision beside the evidence. Handoffs and the decision log moved to their own tabs.
2The warning banner was a full-width paragraph of orange text above everything.It was read once and then ignored, and it pushed the work down.One short line, kept visible, in plain words.
3Labels in capitals ("1 · PROJECT TO SCAN", "STEP 5"), mixed pill colours and decorative borders competed for attention.Colour should mean something.Sentence case. Colour is used only for state: waiting for you, verified, at risk.
4The submit button was far from the decisions and always enabled.A person could finish a review without deciding on every fix.A sticky bar shows "2 of 3 decided" and enables Finish review only when every fix has a decision.
5Model settings (endpoint, key, agents, acknowledgement) were a long form in the left column.Most people never touch them.A compact Agents panel. The form opens on demand.
6The docs were two very long pages. The prompts page showed the prompt, the output, two columns and a checklist for every step at once, on a dense, sidebar-heavy layout.Nobody could see the shape of the project.A short Guide, a collapsed list of the fifteen prompts, and a filterable list of the 21 test cases. Detail opens on demand.
7The docs were not reachable from the console.The person who runs a scan could not find the explanation.A top navigation on every page, and contextual links ("How to connect a model").
8Several internal terms leaked into the interface ("handoff", "x-owner", "recheck").They mean nothing to a new user.Plain labels: "Temporary copy", "Originals intact?", "You decide". Internal terms stay in the Handoffs tab.

Design choices

  • One accent colour (deep teal) and a calm cool grey ground. Amber means "waiting for you", green means verified, red means risk.
  • The pipeline strip is the one distinctive element: seven steps on a single line, so you always know where the scan is and who is acting.
  • Typefaces: Public Sans for text and JetBrains Mono for code. Body text stays under about 70 characters a line.
  • Accessibility: visible keyboard focus, labels on every input, a light and a dark theme, reduced-motion respected, layout works at phone width.

Still to do

  • Test with real users: can someone who has never seen it finish a review without help?
  • The diff view is unified only. A side-by-side view would help with larger patches.
  • Large projects need pagination in the findings list.
